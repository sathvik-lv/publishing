A jewellery business, end to end, on one computer

Billing, stock, the workshop, GST returns and double-entry books for a working jewellery shop — built as a desktop application that runs with the network cable out and costs nothing to operate. Eleven departments, 281 endpoints and 1,538 tests. No subscriptions, no server, no AI in the product.

01What it actually does

Not a dashboard over someone else's system — it is the system. A piece is tagged when it arrives, priced from the rate fixed that morning, sold at the counter, posted to the books, and reported to the GST portal, without leaving the application.

02The rules it refuses to break

Most of the engineering here is not features — it is making the shop's own rules impossible to violate, including by a future version of the software. Each of these is enforced in the service layer and covered by tests that fail if the rule is loosened.

No customer credit, ever. A bill cannot be posted while anything is unpaid — there is no override parameter and no counter it can be switched off for. "Credit" was also removed as a tender, because paying by credit would have zeroed the balance without money arriving.
The accounts are the owner's alone. Any attempt to grant a finance permission to a role other than owner is refused outright, rather than being a convention someone can click past.
One making charge, one gem price. Making is the shop's fixed percentage on everything, whatever a piece cost to buy; a gem prices at the rate in the stone master, not what that parcel cost. Neither can be typed per piece — a better price for one customer is a recorded discount at the till, not a quiet edit.
Weights are derived, never typed. Gross, net and stone weight are computed from the piece's own priced lines — metal in grams, stones in carats, converted at the 1907 definition. The lines are the truth; the scale reading is a check against them.
Cash over two lakh is refused under section 269ST, and cash payments over ten thousand to one person in a day are flagged with their tax cost under 40A(3). The refusal happens at the till, where it can still be acted on.
Document numbers are gapless per branch, per Indian financial year, allocated under a row lock at the moment of posting — so an abandoned draft leaves no hole for an auditor to ask about.
Nothing financial is edited — only reversed. Posted vouchers are corrected by a reversing entry, the audit log is an append-only hash chain, and the metal ledger is signed and append-only in fine weight.
03The application

Screens below run on a synthetic demo dataset — a generated year of trading, not the shop's real books. The interface is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework and no build step, and every chart is drawn with CSS and inline SVG rather than a charting library, because the whole thing ships as one downloadable binary with no network.

04How it is built
DecisionWhy
Desktop app, not a service A shop should not lose its billing system because a card expired or the internet is down during a wedding season. It installs under the user's own account, needs no administrator rights, and holds its data in one file that a backup can carry.
Money and weight as integers Paise and milligrams in scaled integers through a SQLAlchemy type, with floats rejected at the boundary. Money that drifts by a paisa per bill is money that will not reconcile at year end.
SQLite, Postgres-compatible One shop with one till is comfortably a SQLite workload — 60 bills a day for ten years is about 150k lines. The schema avoids Postgres-only types, so scaling up is a connection-string change and nothing else.
No LLM anywhere in the product A bill has to be reproducible and explainable to a tax officer. Every figure comes from arithmetic that can be pointed at in the source.
Vanilla JS, no build step No node_modules, no toolchain to drift between machines, and the shipped JavaScript is the JavaScript that was written.
Barcodes written from scratch Code 128 subset B is 144 lines, checksum included. A dependency for it would have been a dependency to keep current for the next decade.
Branches converge by file Each branch keeps its own hash-chained run of events and they meet by exchanging a bundle. A bundle carries the shop's own key, so another business's file is refused rather than merged into the books.
05What it is not
  1. It is not a product for sale. This was built for one specific shop, and the rules above are that shop's rules — the fixed making percentage, the refusal of credit, the owner-only accounts. Another jeweller would want several of them to be settings.
  2. The builds are unsigned. Windows SmartScreen and macOS Gatekeeper both warn on first run. Clearing that needs a paid certificate on each platform, which is a deliberate non-decision rather than an oversight.
  3. The screenshots are synthetic data. A generated year of trading is used for development and for these images. Real books are never published, and the source repository is private.
  4. Multi-branch is built but lightly exercised. The sync engine, its hash chain and the shop-key check are tested, but they have not yet run for a year across two real showrooms, which is the only test that finally counts.
