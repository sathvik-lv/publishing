03Validation — Is the Timing Real?
The test that matters. Gold rose roughly 23× in rupee terms over this window, so almost any long-biased rule shows a profit. The question is whether the timing adds anything beyond simply being in gold. To test it, 2,000 random-entry strategies were run with the same number of trades and the same holding-period distribution — differing only in when they traded.

The strategy beats 100.0% of random-entry simulations (p = 0.0000). The single luckiest of 2,000 random attempts does not come close.
Exposure-matched control. The book is in the market only 49.9% of the time. Holding a constant 49.9% gold position over the same window returns +7.37% CAGR at Sharpe 0.795. The timing therefore contributes +21.9 pp of CAGR and +0.91 of Sharpe over passively holding the same average exposure.
Bootstrap precision. Resampling the 995 individual trades (n=2,000) gives a 95% confidence interval on CAGR of 22.65% – 36.30%, with P(worse than buy & hold) = 0%.

Selection discipline. Parameters were chosen using only a training window and then scored on data the search never touched. An earlier iteration of this study selected on the full sample and reported a materially better out-of-sample figure; that result was discarded as contaminated. The numbers here are from the corrected procedure.

✓ The bear market is the load-bearing evidence

Gold fell roughly 45% in USD between 2011 and 2015 and did not recover until 2020. A long-only gold rule tested exclusively on bull markets proves nothing. Over that four-year decline the book returned +100.31% against buy & hold’s −19.19%. The book is selective rather than continuously invested, so in an extended decline capital spends most of its time flat instead of absorbing the drawdown. Extending the window back to 2003 specifically to capture this regime was the single most important robustness step in the study.

06Adjusting for Real-World Frictions

⚠ This runs on a synthetic series, not MCX contract prices

The backtest prices XAU/INR as international gold × USD/INR. Indian domestic gold additionally carries import duty and GST, trades at a local premium or discount that moves daily, and is accessed through expiring futures contracts. MCX historical data could not be obtained programmatically — the exchange blocks automated access — so the sensitivity below estimates how much of the result would survive rather than measuring it directly.

Basis noise dominates, not roll cost — the daily premium of domestic gold over the reference series is uninformative to the rule and cannot be diversified away. Roll transaction cost is comparatively minor, since holding periods are short enough that most positions never span a contract expiry. The July 2024 import-duty cut (15% → 6%) costs a one-off 0.57 pp and does not compound, because duty is constant either side of it. On the central estimate the edge over buy & hold survives; on the worst case it does not.
07Construction — High Level
Instrument. Gold denominated in Indian rupees, daily bars. The book is long or flat only — never short. A symmetric short book was built and tested, and was rejected: it lost in every paired comparison and failed even when restricted to confirmed downtrends. Rupee gold carried a structural upward drift across the window, and fighting it proved uneconomic.

Signal. A small number of conditions, all computed strictly from information available at the prior close. Signals are evaluated on that close and executed at the following session’s open, so no bar can act on its own outcome. Positions are closed on a defined exit condition or a profit target, whichever comes first. Signal inputs, lookbacks, thresholds, target sizing and exit logic are held in the private research repository and are not disclosed here.

Risk management. The book runs without a hard stop-loss. This was not an omission — a sweep across 17 stop levels and five independent windows found none worth adopting on risk-adjusted return. Tight stops in particular were actively destructive, cutting normal fluctuation rather than tail risk. Wide stops behave as disaster insurance only, buying a materially smaller worst-case trade for a modest cost in annual return; whether that trade is worth making is a risk-tolerance decision rather than a statistical one.

Costs. 16 bp round trip applied throughout. This is deliberately conservative: the equivalent all-in charge on Indian gold futures, including brokerage, exchange fees, commodities transaction tax, GST, stamp duty and slippage, works out several times smaller. Results are therefore understated on the cost dimension.

Search discipline. An initial sweep of 1,146 configurations across trend, breakout, market-structure, mean-reversion and machine-learning families produced nothing that survived a deflated-Sharpe test, and none beat buy & hold out of sample. That null result is the reason the surviving rule is deliberately small: complexity was tested extensively and did not pay. Several individually plausible filters were measured and dropped for reducing returns rather than improving them.

⚠ Caveats — Read These

  1. Not the tradeable instrument. The series is international gold × USD/INR, not MCX contract prices. Indian domestic gold carries import duty, GST, a daily-varying local premium, and contract expiries. Section 06 estimates the impact; it has not been measured. The central estimate after adjustment is roughly 22%, not 29%.
  2. Backtested, not live. No capital has been deployed against this rule. Figures are a historical simulation.
  3. Long-biased in a rising market. Rupee gold appreciated at roughly 14.6% per year across the window. A long-only book inherits that. The exposure-matched and random-entry controls in section 03 exist precisely to separate skill from that drift, but they cannot remove the dependence on gold being an appreciating asset over the next two decades.
  4. Negative skew. The win rate is high while the average loss slightly exceeds the average win. Sharpe flatters this shape. A cluster of reversal exits will hurt more than the headline ratios imply; the worst single trade in the record is −15.25%.
  5. Futures introduce leverage by default. On Indian gold futures a single standard mini lot exceeds the notional most retail accounts hold, and margin is roughly 6% of contract value. Position size must be set from notional, not from margin. At maximum available leverage a 6% adverse move is a total loss.
  6. Multiple-testing exposure. Over a thousand configurations were evaluated across this project. The reported rule passes a deflated-Sharpe test that penalises for that search, and an earlier contaminated result was discarded, but the risk of selection bias never reaches zero.
  7. Data provenance. Price history was cross-checked against a second, independently sourced vendor series: price levels agreed to a 0.19% median deviation, but daily returns disagreed by roughly 52 bp, traceable almost entirely to differing rupee-conversion conventions between the two providers. That disagreement sets a floor below which no edge can honestly be claimed, and is the basis for the noise sensitivity in section 06.
